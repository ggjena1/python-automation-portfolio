
What You Missed (Hidden Gems in Lists)

To make your list knowledge truly complete, here are 4 critical concepts you should add to your roadmap:
• Memory Management (Shallow vs. Deep Copy): Knowing the difference between copying a list using new_list = old_list (which connects them) vs old_list.copy() (which separates them).
• List Methods for Ordering: Understanding .sort() (changes the list permanently) vs sorted() (creates a new sorted list), and .reverse().
• Searching & Counting: Using .index(item) to find a position, .count(item) to count duplicates, and the in keyword to check if an item exists.
• Performance / Time Complexity: Understanding why adding/removing items at the end of a list is lightning fast, but adding/removing at the beginning slows Python down.




 Missing Gems in Dictionaries (To add to your roadmap)

• The .get() Method: Learning why my_dict.get('key') is safer than my_dict['key'] (it prevents your program from crashing if the key doesn't exist).
• Dictionary Views (.keys(), .values(), .items()): How to extract just the keys, just the values, or both together.
• Merging Dictionaries: Using the union operator (|) or .update() to combine two dictionaries.

Given a deeply nested structure data = {'user': {'profile': {'name': 'Alice'}}}, how can you safely retrieve 'name' without crashing if 'profile' might be missing?
A. 
B. data.get('user', {}).get('profile', {}).get('name')
Correct! Chaining .get() with empty fallback dictionaries ({}) ensures that every step of the nested lookup fails safely if a parent key is missing.
